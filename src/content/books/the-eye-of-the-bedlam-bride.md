Carl and Donut are sent back to a captured version of Earth from before the crawl, where they have to return to a place from their past and face whatever is waiting there. On top of that, a massive demon eviction event throws everything into chaos, and the dungeon finds new ways to threaten Carl from the inside.

As the verdict says, this is the book where they face their past, both the parts they know and the parts they don't. Carl is my favourite character again, and seeing him walk through a ghost of the world he lost gives the book a quiet sadness the series hasn't had before. The image of "that lonely dog, left alone to guard an empty gas station on Christmas day" is the one that stuck with me most. The title's warning about obsession, "Never stare into the blinding eye of the Bedlam Bride", ties the whole book together nicely. And there are still plenty of laughs, like Mordecai's advice that mages can't cast spells "when you're punching them in the face."

With a trip into the past and a giant demon event at the same time, it sometimes feels like two books competing for space, and a couple of threads move quickly. Even so, it's one of the most personal books in the series and a Must Read for anyone following Carl and Donut.
