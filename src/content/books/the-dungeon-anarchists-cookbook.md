reviewText: |
  The Apocalypse will be telivised 
  The third book leans into the strange guidebook that gives it its name: a cookbook of tips, tricks and explosive recipes left behind by the crawlers who came before Carl. It's the perfect excuse for even bigger and more dangerous plans, and before long everyone is in a bloody mess and neck deep in trouble.

  What I loved most is how much this book is about needing other people. Carl, my favourite character again, has felt alone for most of his life, and seeing him finally realise at the edge of the apocalypse how much he depends on the people around him is one of my favourite moments in the series. Donut gets some lovely, loyal moments too ("Who do you think I am? Miss Beatrice?"), and the humour is as sharp as ever, right down to a robot offering to translate things into "earth monkey speak." There's real darkness as well: the line about parents who "cast a shadow so thick, you can drown in it" hit me harder than I expected.

  If I had one complaint, it's that the stat boxes and loot lists can still slow things down, and the violence is bloodier than ever. If you're already hooked on Carl and Donut, this is where the story gets its claws into you, and it's a Must Read.
